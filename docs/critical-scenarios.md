# Critical phone-failure scenarios

AGENT-PHONE is most useful where a handheld phone has the wrong physical interaction model, not merely where a smaller screen is convenient.

The matrix below is deliberately skeptical: every advantage is paired with the new risk introduced by a wrist computer, ring, glasses, audio, or body-worn battery. “Screen-off” describes the path that should remain usable while the OLED is black.

| Scenario | Why a phone fails | AGENT advantage | Screen-off path | Accessory dependency | New risk / constraint | Evidence / reference |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Industrial / frontline work | Tools, ladders, machinery, manuals and flashlights already occupy both hands. | Wrist glance + voice/silent input + ring keeps both hands on the task. | Haptic alerts, private audio, ring confirmation. | Ring or glasses helpful, not required. | Any display can still distract around machinery; actions need lockouts. | https://www.realwear.com/blog/your-most-important-tools-are-your-hands-what-is-hands-free-operation |
| Warehouse / picking / inventory | Repeatedly pulling out a phone slows scan-sort-carry loops. | Wrist computer remains present while hands pick, sort and scan. | Haptics + scanner/ring confirmation. | Scanner/ring optional. | Cuff must survive impact, cold and cleaning. | https://www.zebra.com/us/en/products/mobile-computers/wearable-computers/wt6400.html |
| Cycling / scooter navigation | Holding or typing on a phone reduces peripheral performance and increases workload. | Minimal glance navigation, haptic turns and private audio reduce head-down interaction. | Turn haptics + audio only. | Glasses/audio optional. | Must never encourage messaging while moving; HUD clutter can distract. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20069479/ |
| Car HUD | Phone navigation competes for a head-down glance and often sits off the forward sightline. | AGENT can hand navigation, calls and critical status to the vehicle HUD automatically while the wrist screen stays dark. | Audio + haptic confirmations; HUD only for sparse driving-relevant cues. | Compatible vehicle HUD. | HUD complexity can cause attentional tunneling; non-driving notifications must be suppressed. | https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37287261/ |
| Healthcare / clinical work | Phones are repeatedly handled around gloves, patients and shared surfaces. | Washable cuff plus voice/silent/ring input can reduce phone handling. | Haptics + private audio + silent articulation. | Ring/audio optional. | Wearable is not sterile by default and needs cleaning protocols. | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10552405/ |
| Cold / gloves / snow | Capacitive touch and pocket access become awkward with gloves. | Ring, sEMG, wrist motion, voice and haptics provide redundant controls. | Fully possible for short actions. | Ring improves reliability. | Sensors must distinguish deliberate gestures from cold tremor/motion. | https://support.apple.com/102567 |
| Rain / wet or dirty hands | A wet touchscreen is unreliable and the phone itself becomes another contaminated surface. | Gesture/ring/voice paths avoid touching the screen. | Haptic + audio confirmation. | None required. | Water sealing and accidental gestures need validation. | https://support.apple.com/102567 |
| Cooking / food prep | Hands are wet, oily or contaminated and a phone occupies counter space. | Timers, recipe steps, calls and appliance control stay on wrist. | Audio next-step + ring/gesture. | Audio/ring optional. | Hygiene and heat exposure require material limits. | Product hypothesis. |

| Diving / boating | A normal phone is inaccessible underwater and awkward with wet hands. | Dive cuff can become the primary status/navigation surface with haptics and physical/gesture fallbacks. | Haptics + minimal emissive status. | Dive clasp / sensors. | Waterproofing, pressure, gloves and false inputs are safety-critical. | https://www.garmin.com/en-US/p/852159 |
| Climbing / rescue / field repair | Letting go of an anchor, tool or rope to retrieve a phone is unsafe. | Wrist status + private audio + ring can keep the task hand available. | Navigation/SOS/audio/haptic. | Ring/glasses useful. | Gestures must not trigger from climbing motions; impact resistance matters. | Product hypothesis informed by hands-free industrial systems. |
| Carrying a child / groceries / luggage | One or both hands are already occupied. | Messaging, navigation, calls, access and payment remain available without retrieving anything. | Voice/silent reply + haptic confirmation. | None required. | Accidental child contact and privacy in public audio. | Product hypothesis. |
| Cane / crutch / temporary injury | A phone assumes one free dexterous hand. | Gaze, speech, ring, wrist gestures and silent articulation are redundant input channels. | Audio/haptic path can avoid touch entirely. | Depends on individual ability. | Accessibility cannot depend on one gesture vocabulary; must be configurable. | https://support.apple.com/en-us/111111 |
| Limited dexterity / limb difference | Small phone targets and two-hand workflows can be inaccessible. | Multiple sensing modalities can be personalized per user. | Haptics + audio + chosen gesture channel. | Optional ring/glasses. | Biosignal models need inclusive training and calibration. | https://support.apple.com/en-us/111111 |
| Crowded transit / theft-prone places | Taking out a large valuable phone exposes it and consumes a grip hand. | Glanceable wrist status reduces visible handheld-device time. | Haptics + private audio. | None required. | A visible wrist computer can itself attract theft; quick lock is required. | Product hypothesis. |
| Standing bus/train | One hand may be on a rail and the other carrying a bag. | Tilted wrist screen can be read without giving up balance. | Audio/haptic for simple actions. | None required. | Motion increases input noise and reading time. | Product hypothesis. |
| Night / bed / dark room | A bright phone screen lights the room and requires visual attention. | Silent composition works with almost all pixels off. | Tiny status pixels + haptic + private TTS readback. | Audio optional but valuable. | Audio can leak; haptics must be subtle. | Product hypothesis. |
| Meetings / face-to-face conversation | Pulling out a phone is visually disruptive and signals disengagement. | Brief wrist glance or silent reply can reduce visible screen time. | Silent articulation + haptic/TTS. | Audio optional. | Wearable checking can still be socially distracting. | Product hypothesis. |
| Spatial home/device control | Phone remotes require unlock → app → device → control. | Point ring/glasses at the TV, light or speaker, then manipulate directly. | Ring haptic confirms target and action. | Ring + optional glasses/UWB. | Wrong-device selection needs explicit confirmation. | See docs/ring-and-spatial-input.md |
| Cross-device content handoff | Phone share sheets force app-centric routing. | Grab/select an entity and hand it to TV, car HUD, glasses or another AGENT. | Haptic target preview + release gesture. | Target display/device. | Accidental disclosure to the wrong nearby device. | See docs/agent-os.md |
| Emergency quick actions | Unlocking and navigating apps wastes time when stressed or injured. | SOS, medical ID, navigation, door/light and calls can live in the always-on path. | Dedicated gesture + haptic/audio confirmation. | None required. | False emergency activation and authentication trade-offs. | Product requirement. |
| Camera / live assistance | A phone must be held, propped up or surrendered as the viewfinder. | Detachable camera tile or glasses can stream POV while both hands work; wrist is the controller/viewfinder. | Ring shutter + audio cues. | Camera tile or glasses. | Camera privacy and obvious recording state are mandatory. | See docs/camera-strategy.md |

## Design consequences

The strongest scenarios converge on the same architecture: the wrist computer is always present; the main OLED is dark much of the time; haptics/private audio handle confirmation; the ring and deliberate silent articulation provide input without occupying the display; and larger visual work moves to the nearest trusted screen.

Car HUD is therefore a first-class handoff target, not an automotive app running on the wrist. While driving, the system should expose only driving-relevant navigation, vehicle and urgent communication cues. Research on automotive HUDs is mixed: some studies show better forward-view attention allocation, while others show attentional tunneling from visually complex HUDs. AGENT should use the HUD sparsely and suppress ordinary notifications.
